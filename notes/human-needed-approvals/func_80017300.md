## Read the score strictly

`diffFunc` re-aligns the two instruction streams with an LCS before comparing,
so a transposed *pair* produces one structural row and **zero** "differing"
rows. For this object it reports `320/331, 7 differing`; the index-by-index
count is `318/331, 13 differing`. Four of the residual sites are transpositions
and are invisible in the headline number. Quote the strict count.

## Where it stands

The function is a raw-compression sprite decompressor: per entry it decodes
either an RLE row stream, an RLE two-channel column stream, or a raw byte row
stream into `D_8005EE28`, optionally ORs 0x8000 into every pixel after the
first, then `LoadImage` + `DrawSync` per scanline.

Semantics are settled. Instruction count (331), opcode multiset, the inventory,
and every register web except three are exact. The whole loop head, all three

## The 13 remaining words — three coupled compiler-state ties

### 1. Branch A and C preheader order (4 words)

Target emits `[lui %hi(D_8005EE28), andi s6,a0,2, move s7,t0]`; the attempt
emits `[andi, lui, move]`.

`schedulerTrace --pass sched2` shows all three insns **tie on priority** (all
1 — `insn_cost` is 0 on r3000 for these, so a dependence chain contributes
nothing), and `rank_for_schedule` breaks ties by descending LUID while
scheduling bottom-up, which reproduces the incoming order exactly. **The
scheduler is not the actor.** The order is `loop.c`'s movable-emission order,
which is the loop body's RTL order (`scan_loop` appends via
`last_movable->next`; `move_movables` emits each with
`emit_insn_before (..., loop_start)`).

**What produces each insn (verified in the `.gcse` and `.loop` dumps):**

```
PRE: redundant insn 366 (expression 38) in bb 22, reaching reg is 274
PRE/HOIST: end of bb 9,  insn 1032, copying expression 38 to reg 274
```

Expression 38 is `high(D_8005EE28)`. `pre_insert` walks blocks and inserts at
the **end** of every block where `OPTIMAL & ~REDUNDANT`, in **ascending
expression index**. `insert_insn_end_bb` puts the insn before the block's
closing jump — i.e. after every original insn in the block.

- **Branch B is correct** because there *both* preheader insns are PRE
  insertions into the same block, emitted in index order:
  ```
  PRE/HOIST: end of bb 27, insn 1041, copying expression 38 to reg 274   <- lui
  PRE/HOIST: end of bb 27, insn 1044, copying expression 40 to reg 277   <- andi
  ```
  Its `flags2 = entry_data & 2` sits at the use site, and PRE's lazy placement
  walks it back to the row-loop top because the *channel loop* head is a join
  whose `DELAYIN` is 0, which stops the delay chain there.
- **Branch A and C** have no such nested loop between the row-loop top and the
  flag test. Putting `flags2` at the use site there yields **two** insertions:
  ```
  PRE/HOIST: end of bb 9,  insn 1032, expression 38 -> reg 274
  PRE/HOIST: end of bb 9,  insn 1035, expression 54 -> reg 277   <- wanted
  PRE/HOIST: end of bb 10, insn 1038, expression 54 -> reg 277   <- fatal
  ```
  bb 10 is the one-insn block holding `row = row_width`. Two sets of reg 277 in
  the loop means `set_in_loop != 1`, so `scan_loop` never lists it and the
  `andi` stays inside the loop (measured: 328 instructions, 24/331).

**Why bb 10 always gets the second insertion.** `pre_lcm` is called without the
`comp` bitmap, and `compute_latein` degenerates to `LATEIN == DELAYIN` for
every block but the last. Then
`DELAYOUT(bb) = ~ANTLOC(bb) & DELAYIN(bb)`, so with no `and` in bb 9 the delay
flows into bb 10, and `OPTIMAL(bb10) = DELAYIN(bb10) & ~ISOOUT(bb10) = 1`.
Suppressing it needs `DELAYOUT(bb9) = 0`, i.e. `ANTLOC(bb9) = 1`, i.e. an
*original* `and` in bb 9 — which `loop.c` then hoists **before** the PRE
insertion, which is the failure we started from.

**Deleting bb 10 does work, and is measured.** Moving `row = row_width` above
the guard removes the block, and the `.gcse` dump then shows exactly the wanted
pair in all three row-loop-top blocks (bb 9, bb 26, bb 45) and nothing at
bb 10/bb 47. It is still a net loss:
- the guard then tests `row` instead of `row_width`, dropping `row_width`'s
  `REG_N_REFS` from 19 to 13 (two references at row-loop depth), which drops
  its allocno priority from 4108 to 2108 and rotates `$s4`/`$s6`/`$s5`
  block-wide (~20 words);
- the loop-top block emission changes from the target's
  `[addiu dst, beqz s4, addiu s2,a1,1 (delay)] + [move a1,s4]` to
  `[addiu s2,a1,1, move a1,s4, beqz, addiu dst (delay)]` (4 words per branch).
  The best reachable form of that shape still costs 2 words per branch, which
  is exactly what the preheader transposition costs — the trade can never be
  better than a wash.

**The closed argument.** For the `and` to follow the `high` in the movable list
it must be either
(i) a PRE insertion at the end of bb 9 — excluded above, or
(ii) an original insn in a block after bb 9 — excluded because `maybe_never` is
1 for every block after the guard jump, so `scan_loop` requires
`reg_in_basic_block_p`, which requires the value's **last use** to be in the
same block as its set; `flags2`'s use is the flag test, always a later block.
Every source shape tried lands in one of those two buckets. Either the target's
CFG differs from this reconstruction in some way not yet identified, or there
is a third `loop.c`/`gcse.c` path not covered by this reading.

### 2. `count_rle = 0` / `repeat = 0` at the join block (3 words)

`reorg`'s `fill_slots_from_thread` steals the join block's **first** insn into
the 0x800173FC delay slot and redirects the branch past it, so the target's
`move s1,zero` at 0x80017400 and 0x80017424 proves `count_rle = 0` is written
first. Writing it first does produce that emission — and costs the allocation.

`allocno_compare` (global.c) ranks by
`(int)(floor_log2(n_refs) * n_refs / live_length * 10000 * size)`:

| pseudo | role | n_refs | live_length | priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 108 | `repeat`, written second | 29 | 544 | **2132** |
| 150 | branch-A `high(D_8005EE28)` | 8 | 114 | 2105 |
| 108 | `repeat`, written first | 29 | 552 | 2101 |

At 544 `repeat` outranks pseudo 150, takes `$s7` first and pushes it to `$s8`;
`greg` confirms the whole regression is that one swap (`108: 30->23`,
`150: 23->30`, `189: 23->30`) and nothing else. Closing it needs
`live_length(108) >= 552` or `live_length(150) <= 112` or
`n_refs(150) >= 9`, with the emission unchanged. Swept and inert: `repeat` and
`count_rle` types, five declaration positions, **all twelve** orderings of
`{count_rle, repeat, row_width, height}` with `count_rle` first, and splitting
`repeat` into per-branch variables (needs its own initialiser: 334 insns).

**The near miss — start here next time.** Writing `repeat = 0;` *before* the
x/y-adjust if/else and `count_rle = 0;` after it **keeps the correct
allocation** (`repeat` is born early enough for `live_length` 552+) **and**
gets the join block right: `move s1,zero` lands at both 0x80017400 and
0x80017424, and every downstream word in the whole function falls into place.
It fails on exactly one instruction: `repeat = 0` is then emitted at
0x8001738C, in the loop-head block, instead of at 0x80017428. `sched2`
schedules it there because it is a leaf (no dependents in that block) and gets
picked early in the bottom-up fill; source position inside the block does not
move it. LCS score 321 same / 7 differing — the best either metric has seen —
but the single displaced insn shifts every following address, so the strict
score is 281/331.

That is the whole of residual 2 reduced to one question: **can `repeat = 0` be
born before the if/else while still being emitted in the join block?** A
duplicate store in every arm was tried (`count_rle = 0` written into all three
paths, `repeat = 0` in the join) and cross-jumping does not reassemble it the
way the target does: 68/331, 330 instructions.

### 3. Branch A's constant 1 and the arg5 reload registers (6 words)

## Mechanisms that DID resolve (keep these)

- **Branch B: `channel = 0;` before `next_row = row + 2;`.** Both are tied
  leaves in the row-loop-top block, so sched2 keeps source order. Worth 2 words.
- `flags2 = entry_data & 2` must reach the loop preheader. At the top of the
  row-loop body `loop.c` always hoists it; at the use site only gcse-PRE can
  relocate it, and only where the delay chain stops at the row-loop top.
- **Split `header` from the flags variable.** Keeping the raw word in its own
  local is what holds the 0xFFFFFF constant live in `$v0` across the `rect.h`
  load, which forces that load into `$v1` and pins `sh v1,22(sp)` ahead of
  `andi v1,a0,3`. This single change fixed the entire loop-head cluster.
- **Declaration order is not inert.** It sets pseudo numbers, which set the
  gcse expression-hash bucket order for `size + 3` versus `entry_idx + 1`, which
  decides which of the two owns caller-save slot 0x2C versus 0x30.
  `entry_idx` must be declared at least three slots after `size`. Worth 8 words.
- A **fresh `bytes` local** for the rounded byte count, rather than assigning
  back into `size`, restores the target's single-`$v0` tail chain.
- `rect.h = 1;` before `row = 0;` (natural `for`-init order).

## Reusable facts established here

- **`REG_N_REFS` weighting is `sum of loop_depth over references`, function body
  = 1 and each loop nesting +1.** Verified twice against the `.lreg` dump:
  `repeat` = 2 (entry-loop init) + 3x4 (branch A RLE) + 3x5 (branch B RLE,
  one deeper for the channel loop) = 29; the branch-A `%hi` pseudo = 2 (set in
  the row-loop preheader) + 3 (loop-top use) + 3 (flag-pass use) = 8. This makes
  allocno priorities computable by hand from the source shape.
- **In GCC 2.95, `pre_insert` emits several insertions into one block in
  ascending expression-hash index.** When two values are both PRE-relocated to
  the same preheader, their order is the hash-table order, not the source order.
- **`register T x asm("$30")` does not reserve the register in this compiler.**
  The probe emitted `lb $16,0($fp)` — `src` was allocated to the pinned
  register. Hard-register pinning has no purchase on allocation residuals here,
  independent of policy.

## Ruled out

- Exhaustive residual search: `searchResidualSourceSpace --derive-only` prices
  the domain at 2.14e15 candidates. Not an option until the residual is smaller.
- Row loop written as `while (row < height)` instead of the guarded do-while:
  byte-identical output (jump.c converts it back), 318/331.
- Inner RLE loop written as `while`: worse.
- `flags2` computed inside the RLE loop body, or in the `row = row_width`
  block: never hoisted (`maybe_never` + `reg_in_basic_block_p`), 83/331.
- A single pre-dispatch flag computation: LCM leaves it where it is
  (`DELAYOUT = 0` at its own block), so it stays one `andi`, not three.
- Natural `for`-loop rewrites of the row/column loops: the hand-rolled
  `next_row` carry is what reproduces the target's `addiu s2,a1,1` in the inner
  preheader plus `addu a1,s2,zero` at the bottom.
- Type changes on `repeat` / `flags2` (s32, u32): inert.
- An explicit `u16 *` base in the pixel-flag loop: costs 94 words.

## The one lever found but NOT exhausted

`scan_loop` does not always build the movable list in stream order. If the
loop's first in-range insn is an unconditional jump to a label inside the loop
("entered near the bottom"), it sets `scan_start = JUMP_LABEL (p)` and
`loop_top = next_label (scan_start)`, after which `next_insn_in_loop` scans
`[scan_start .. end]` and then wraps to `[loop_top .. scan_start]`. That
reorders the movable list relative to the RTL stream — **the exact degree of
freedom the closed argument above says does not otherwise exist**, and the only
thing that could put the `and` after the PRE-inserted `lui` while keeping bb 10.

Caveats before spending a session on it: `scan_loop` rejects the loop as
"phony" unless `scan_start` is a `CODE_LABEL` with UID below
`max_uid_for_loop`, and the shape implies an entry jump the target does not
appear to have. It was found too late in the session to test. Full quote and
reasoning in the research note, section 8.
