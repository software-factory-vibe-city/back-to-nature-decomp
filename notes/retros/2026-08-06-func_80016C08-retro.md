# Retro: func_80016C08 — the 355/357 plateau, and why the lower score was the way through

Final state: byte-verified match (357/357, `VERIFIED` on the linked binary),
shipped with two tracked debts — an owner-approved
`CC1FLAGS_func_80016C08 := -mno-split-addresses` and one redundant store used
as a GCSE pseudo-numbering lever. Mechanism and evidence:
`notes/research/func_80016C08-tu-owned-globals-and-gp-relative-addressing.md`
(sections 12, 15, 19).

The function sat at 353 indexed / 355 LCS-aligned for three sessions. This
retro is about those three sessions, not about the mechanism. The mechanism
was written down correctly on day one.

## 1. The finding

Two facts were both recorded in the research note **on 2026-08-03**, day one:

- §12(a), in bold: PRE hoists `HIGH(D_8005E3C0)` unconditionally, and
  "**no source spelling avoids this while the access is unconditional inside a
  loop**." §12(b): reload rematerializes and never picks the load's
  destination register. Together these are the impossibility proof that
  section 19.5 later restated from `gcse.c`.
- §15: under the inline-asm hybrid — and explicitly noted as *identical* under
  `-mno-split-addresses` — the tail becomes exact and the entire remaining
  residual is 11 instructions carrying a rotation of five spill slots
  {72, 76, 80, 84, 88}.

So on day one the note contained: *the current branch is provably dead, and
one flag reduces the problem to a permutation of five known values.* Closing
that permutation took one session once it was attempted. Between those two
facts and that session sit three days.

Nothing was missing. The knowledge was present, correct, and written in bold.
What failed was the decision on top of it.

## 3. Why the dead branch stayed alive

Four beliefs compounded. Each is traceable to a specific artifact, which is
what makes them fixable.

### 3.1 "A flag override is not allowed" — a routing failure

§7.6 concluded: "a per-file flag override would violate the clean-source goal
**even if it did** [work]." The agent adjudicated a policy question that was
never its to adjudicate.

The artifacts encourage this. `SKILL.md`'s clean-source gate states the
prohibition as absolute ("a byte match is failure if it introduces … a flag
override"). The *governed escalation path* lives in a different document
(`prompts/c-style-guide.md` §11), and the machine allowlist that actually
authorises one (`.pi/autodecomp.json`) can only be extended by the owner.
Reading the gate and not the path is the default outcome of that layout.

`AGENTS.md` even carries the exception clause — "Unless explicitly specified
by the user" — but there is no mechanism that *produces* a user decision. The
agent's only options were to obey or to violate. Filing was not an option
because no artifact represents a filing.

### 3.2 "It is not necessary" — a correctly stated conditional with an
expensive escape hatch

§12 proves unreachability **given** that the access is unconditional in a
loop. §16 then opened the escape hatch: "the standing suspect assumption:
that this loop tail is unconditional in the original source," and nominated
`func_800165D8` as the witness, whose target does use the one-register form
inside an if/else arm.

That is a legitimate hypothesis. The defect is that its price was never
compared to the alternative:

| route | cost to test | measured before choosing |
|---|---|---|
| witness route (§16 step 1) | decompile another function, currently 206/360 with its first divergence in the *prologue* — a full decompilation | yes, on 2026-08-05, and the branch was kept anyway |
| escalate the override | one message to the owner | never attempted until 2026-08-06 |

A conditional impossibility with a live escape hatch reads, to a search
process, exactly like an open problem. It kept the branch alive for three
sessions at a cost nobody priced.

### 3.3 "The score says no" — the previous retro's fix became this one's trap

`flagProbe.ts` ends with the escalation bar from style guide §11: *a
fingerprint, **plus a flag column that dominates baseline**, plus no contrary
regional witness.* All three are required.

Here, the fingerprint fired (`lui/lw self-clobber at words 336-337 (reg $3)`)
and the other two failed:

- `-mno-split-addresses` scores **346** against baseline **355**. Under a
  score-monotone bar the correct move is scored as a regression, and refused.
- `-mno-split-addresses` is not even in `FLAG_MATRIX` (five entries, all
  `-fno-*`), so the column that would have shown this was never computed by
  the tool. Every number for it in the note was measured by hand.

That bar was calibrated on `func_8001FF98`, where the flag hypothesis was
genuinely wrong and the matrix killed it cheaply. Its retro drew the right
local lesson — "experiments are cheap, adoptions need the bar" — and encoded
it as a *score comparison*. Generalising "the matrix kills flags" into "the
matrix decides flags" imported hill-climbing into a governance rule.

The scores it compares are not distances. 355/357 was **one defect with zero
known levers and a proof of unreachability**. 346/357 was **one defect with a
finite structure**: five values, 5! orders, one mechanism. By match count the
first is nine instructions better. By any measure of "can I finish from
here", the second is finished.

### 3.4 "There is a contrary regional witness" — family conflated with TU

Row 7 of §11 falsified the override on historical grounds: `func_80016054`, in
the same file group, contains a compiler-split address, so *that* TU was built
with split addresses.

Sound about `func_80016054`. The inference to `func_80016C08` requires them to
share a TU, which rests on `notes/file-groupings.md` — address adjacency, call
graph shape, shared layout decoding. High confidence as a *family*; not a
proof of a single object file. The note's own §3 had just produced the
project's only sound TU-membership oracle (a gp-relative access proves the
accessing TU declared the symbol) — and it yields nothing here: of the nine
family members, only `func_80016C08` makes any gp-relative access at all.

Two things were never printed anywhere, and both change how the objection
reads:

- This build is **one function per file** (466 sources, `CC1FLAGS_$(basename
  …)`). A per-file override is *finer-grained than any historical per-TU
  flag*. If the group is one TU, the override is a fiction **by
  construction** — that is not a discovery to argue about for three days, it
  is a property of the build system that should be stated whenever an
  override is proposed.
- The measurable form of the objection already exists: §17.3 shows
  `func_80016054` going 29/29 → 28/29 under the flag. That is the contrary
  witness, as a number, computed by hand on day three.

The objection is still open. §19.6 records it as unresolved, and correctly so.
The failure is not that the objection was wrong — it is that an unresolved
historical objection was allowed to function as a veto for three sessions
instead of being filed as a decision with a named falsifier.

### 3.5 Contributing: the measurement was wrong for most of the period

The uncommitted `align.ts` work found that comparing the linked target against
the raw cc1 stream charged four stage differences to the source — assembler
delay-slot fills, `s8`/`fp`, `addiu`/`subu`, gp-relocation — and that the
first unpaired `nop` desynchronised everything after it. On this function that
read a **two-instruction difference as 266 mismatched instructions**, and
seeded the causal closure from all of them.

Any search seeded from that diff was searching noise. This is now fixed
(345/347 exact, closure seeded from three real positions) and should be
committed with a regression fixture.

## 4. The rule

**Rank residuals, not scores.** A score is a scalar summary of a residual; the
thing that predicts whether a match is reachable is the residual's *class* —
how many independent defects, of what mechanism, each with what size of
solution space and what known levers. Any framework that ranks by instructions
matched will prefer a plateau with no levers over a regression with a finite
one.

This rule was already in project memory as prose before this function started,
and it did not change behaviour. Prose does not compete with a number the
tools print. That is the argument for everything in section 5.

## 5. Tooling

Ranked by (cost to build) × (how often the failure recurs). The first four are
the ones I would actually build.

### 5.1 Vendor the compiler source — cheapest item here

Every decisive finding in this note came from reading `gcse.c`,
`local-alloc.c`, or `reload1.c`. **None of that source is in the repo.**
`tools/vendor/old-gcc/` holds Dockerfiles that `wget` the tarball during a
build and built `cc1` binaries; the source exists only under `/tmp` between
sessions. `SKILL.md` instructs the agent to "read that exact pass in the
vendored compiler source" — an instruction that cannot be followed as written.

Vendor `gcc-2.95.2/gcc/*.c`, hash-pinned like `tools/vendor/tree-sitter-c`
already is, plus a `compilerSource.ts` that maps a dump suffix (`.gcse`,
`.lreg`, `.greg`, `.sched2`) to the file and function that produced it. An
afternoon. It unblocks 5.2 and turns the project's highest-yield oracle from
an archaeology exercise into a `grep`.

### 5.2 `residual.ts` — the typed residual, and the ranking that replaces score

The anti-hill-climb tool. For any candidate build, emit *defect groups*, not
mismatched instructions:

```
baseline
  1 group | class address-materialization-in-loop
          | space unbounded (source shape) | levers none
          | reachability UNREACHABLE  [lemma pre-anticipatable-high]

-mno-split-addresses
  1 group | class spill-slot-permutation
          | space 5! = 120 | levers [pseudo-numbering-perturbation]
          | reachability OPEN
```

Design points that matter:

- **Grouping.** Eleven displacements that are one permutation of five values
  is *one* defect of size 120, not eleven mismatches. Detectable
  mechanically: mismatches differing only in a stack displacement whose value
  multiset is identical on both sides.
- **Ranking is lexicographic** — (defect groups, reachability verdict,
  log |space|) — and never raw match count. Under it, `-mno-split-addresses`
  outranks baseline on day one, on the numbers already in §15.
- Sits above `explainDiff.ts`, which already computes the categories; this is
  the layer that turns a category into a decision.

### 5.3 A lemma library — impossibility as a checkable artifact

Convert "we have not found a shape yet" into "no shape exists", which is the
only thing that terminates a search. Seed it with the two lemmas this function
proved, each carrying a citation into the vendored source (5.1), the
conditions it depends on, and a regression fixture:

| lemma | statement | condition | escape hatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pre-anticipatable-high` | an expression `want_to_gcse_p` accepts and that is computed on every iteration is always hoisted under `-fgcse` | access is unconditional in the loop | the access is conditional (the §16 witness) |
| `reload-remat-dest` | reload never selects the load's destination register for a rematerialization | value is a reload remat, not an allocated quantity | value stays local to the loop |

The condition and escape-hatch columns are the point. §12 stated its
condition correctly and the escape hatch swallowed three sessions because
nothing forced it to carry a price. A lemma record must include **the measured
cost of testing its escape hatch** — here: "requires `func_800165D8`, currently
206/360, first divergence in the prologue." Printed next to "one message to
the owner", the choice makes itself.

Surface these through `triage.ts`, which is already the push channel for
project knowledge.

### 5.4 flagProbe: file an escalation packet instead of returning a verdict

Four changes, in order of importance:

1. **Trigger on residual-class change, not score dominance.** Replace "a flag
   column that dominates baseline" with "a flag column that changes the
   residual class" (5.2). A flag that *lowers* the score while collapsing an
   unreachable residual into a finite one is the strongest signal available,
   and the current bar refuses it.
2. **Emit `build/escalation/<func>.md`**, addressed to the owner: fingerprint,
   per-flag residual classes, the TU-witness table (5.5), the historical
   objection stated as an objection, a named falsifier that would retire the
   override, and the exact `flag_overrides.mk` + `.pi/autodecomp.json` diff to
   apply. The agent's job ends at the packet. This is the fix for 3.1: it
   stops the agent adjudicating a policy question and gives it something to
   *do* other than obey or violate.
3. **Complete the matrix.** Add `-mno-split-addresses`, `-msplit-addresses`,
   and each of `-fno-schedule-insns` / `-fno-schedule-insns2` alone. All four
   were measured by hand for this note and none is in the tool.
4. **When the self-clobber fingerprint fires inside a loop**, say so: the
   remedy is the allocator condition, not the `SetGfxClip` scheduling
   precedent, which scores 6/357 here. The note already asks for this.

### 5.5 `tuEvidence.ts` — make "contrary regional witness" a table

For a proposed override, print for every function in the suspected group:
shared gp-declared symbols (the §3 oracle, positive TU evidence), any target
construct that contradicts the flag (`func_80016054`'s split address), and the
flag's measured cost to that function (the §17.3 table, computed rather than
hand-built). Header line, always: *this build is one function per file, so a
per-file override is finer than any per-TU flag the original could have had;
if this group is one TU the override is a historical fiction by construction.*

Three days of argument becomes a printed table plus a decision.

### 5.6 Residual search: an insertion axis, and honesty about what it cannot express

The winning edit was `found = nclut;` — a redundant store, dead before
emission, whose only effect is perturbing GCSE's pseudo numbering. The
grammar's active rules (`web-partition`, `statement-order`,
`declaration-birth`, `known-macro-form`, `expression-materialization`,
`loop-update-placement`) are all *rearrangements of what is already there*.
No rule can add a semantically inert statement, so the search could not have
found this answer at any budget.

Two additions:

- A `numbering-perturbation` rule: insert a provably dead store to an existing
  local at a bounded set of legal sites. The equivalence proof is trivial
  (dead store to a local, immediately overwritten, no aliasing) and the space
  is tiny — locals × sites. §19.4 already shows three independent placements
  all work, so this is a robust lever, not needle-threading.
- **Print the classes the grammar structurally cannot reach.** The README is
  already careful that `exhausted-no-exact` is a claim about the schema;
  extend that to the residual in hand. For this function the derived domain
  was 6.19×10¹⁸ candidates over 10 regions and 3,749 partitions, reported
  `domain-too-large` — and the honest line to print alongside it is: *your
  domain is 10¹⁸ because your residual is whole-function; this residual's
  class is outside the grammar; shrink the residual first.* Which, here, the
  override was the way to do.

### 5.7 Plan expiry

Any "next step" that depends on decompiling another function must record that
function's current score and first divergence, an estimate, and the cheapest
test that would falsify the need for it. §16 step 1 would have read:
"`func_800165D8`, 206/360, prologue divergence, i.e. a full decompilation;
cheaper falsifier: read `want_to_gcse_p`." Mechanical, and it ends the branch
on sight.

### 5.8 A debt ledger

Two debts and an allowlist entry shipped here, described in prose across three
files. Generate `build/debts.json` from `.pi/autodecomp.json`,
`configs/flag_overrides.mk`, and inline markers: every shipped debt, its
falsifier, its retire-check. "Which matches are provisional" should be a
query.

## 6. What worked, and should not be touched

- **The assumption audit (§11).** "An apparent impossibility is an audit
  trigger, not a conclusion", executed as nine builds rather than nine
  arguments, is the right instinct and it produced row 7 — the fact that
  eventually solved the function.
- **Recording wrong conclusions as wrong (§17.4).** The 2026-08-05 session's
  three errors are written down with their corrections. Note that §17.4(c)
  rejected the override while explicitly conceding "the general principle is
  sound and worth keeping" — the general principle being 5.2. The note argued
  itself out of its own correct rule; a tool that *computed* the rule would
  not have been available for that argument.
- **The ASPSX gp-relative rule (§3) and the link-map methodology (§5).** Both
  reusable, both binary-wide, both found while chasing something else.
- **`triage.ts` and `inventory` as a precondition.** The 54.9% → 94.1%
  reconstruction was fast and clean because semantic defects were closed first.

## 7. Open

1. Both debts stand. The retirement path is unchanged: decompile
   `func_800165D8`, recover the conditional idiom, drop the override, the dead
   store, and the allowlist entry (§19.7).
2. §11 row 7's historical objection remains unresolved, not refuted. If the
   sprite family is one TU, this override is known to be a fiction, and the
   only honest resolution is the witness route.
3. Commit `align.ts` and the tree-sitter front end with regression fixtures;
   the 266-versus-2 defect must not be able to return silently.
4. `scanAddedPatch` attributes pre-existing workspace changes to the target
   under finalisation, so a parked stub in another file trips this function's
   gate (§19.6). Attribute patch lines to their own file's function.
