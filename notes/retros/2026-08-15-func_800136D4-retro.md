# func_800136D4 retro — 140/202 to MATCH (2026-08-15)

Closed the bevel-panel drawer at 202/202 (`diffFunc` MATCH, full binary OK).
The session inherited a BLK-struct `arg0` reconstruction (param-residence
playbook Step 1, HOMED-ARG reading) stuck at 140/202 with a five-register
rotation: target had color-const→s4, temp_s5→s5, temp_s6→s6, arg1ext→s7,
arg2ext→s8($fp); candidate had everything shifted one register down with the
color in $fp.

## The fix (three edits, in decreasing importance)

1. **`u32 *arg0` plain declaration, not the BLK struct.** The HOMED-ARG
   fingerprint (`sw a0,96(sp)` + `lw a0,96(sp)` per call) was a **reload
   spill** of the call-crossing pointer argument, not assign_parms homing.
   This is the playbook's own documented alternative reading; the census had
   been read as an instruction to apply the BLK declaration.
2. Color 0xFDECD2 as a **literal at its four call sites** (CSE builds the
   shared web). Under the corrected frame this matched; the named-variable
   spelling was byte-identical under the old frame and was not re-tested
   separately under the new one.
3. The spilled 0xBA8B47 local **assigned at its first use**, not in its
   declaration's initializer. At-declaration produced a transposed
   interleave of the two prologue constant pairs (199/202); the assignment
   statement's position is the materialization-order lever (one word:
   `ori v0,v0,0x8b47` vs `ori s4,s4,0xecd2` at 0x80013738).

## Why the wrong residence reading was so expensive

Count, opcode multiset, inventory, and (masked) web shapes were all
plausible under the BLK frame; the diff read as a pure allocation rotation,
and every allocator tool downstream analysed that frame without complaint.
The real difference is **which pass creates the 14 `lw a0,96(sp)` loads**:

- BLK declaration → real block-0 RTL loads, visible to sched1 and lreg.
  They lengthen every call-crossing quantity's span, keep the sched1 ready
  lists busy, and add hazard-filler competition in every call group.
- plain declaration → the pseudo simply loses allocation and reload
  materializes the loads **after** lreg. The whole quantity landscape
  (spans, priorities, ready lists) is computed without them.

The local-allocation priority midfield here was within 4% (ts6 1500,
arg1 1480, arg2 1458, sp2C 1444 under the wrong frame), so the ~14-insn
population difference flipped the entire s4–s8 cascade: under the plain
frame arg2ext fails local allocation and global-alloc gives it $fp, freeing
s4 for the color web.

## Mechanisms measured on the way (general, reusable)

- **Instrumented-oracle validation of the local priority model.** The
  qty_compare formula `floor_log2(refs)*refs*10000/(dead-born)` computed
  from the oracle's exact qty events reproduced the observed 15-quantity
  allocation order exactly. Quantities merge through `combine_regs` ties on
  ANY insn whose output can tie a dying input (addiu/sll/sra chains), not
  just copies; merged stats are sums.
- **Large-constant loads are 2-set webs after the sched1 split**
  (`mips.md` large_int define_split reuses match_dup 0), so
  `birthing_insn_p`'s REG_N_SETS==1 boost never applies to them: they
  drift to the top of the block at sched1, maximizing their qty span.
  A call-crossing constant web can therefore never be "born at first use"
  at lreg time under this scheduler.
- **`sched.c` pins an insn below the last call only when its destination
  pseudo has REG_N_CALLS_CROSSED == 0** (anti-dep on last_function_call).
  Constants that cross calls float; single-call-group constants (the
  epilogue 0xF4DFBD/0xF9CF8F pair) inherit their group's priority and stay
  adjacent.
- **Local-alloc never assigns $fp** (eliminables are excluded in
  find_free_reg), so a callee-saved-$fp web in a target is always
  global-alloc output — a hard constraint on which webs failed local
  allocation in the original compile.
- **Oracle schedule edges distort rather than replicate**: an injected
  anti-dependence is honored for legality but priorities are not
  recomputed, so the pinned insn drags its anchor upward instead of
  gaining depth. Useful as a legality probe, not as a counterfactual
  allocator input.
